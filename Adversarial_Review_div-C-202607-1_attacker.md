## Bear case against the M1b fundamental claim

C's fundamental question, as the artifact itself states it, is "whether the event-trading environment is functional, or whether the regime is dominated by macro shocks that override individual-catalyst dynamics." The document then answers that question almost entirely by restating two upstream regime-axis labels — `shock_overlay = acute` and `policy_stance = hawkish` — and asserting, without any C-specific measurement, that they compress "per-name event-reaction dispersion... toward common macro factors" and degrade "the clean idiosyncratic options pricing C's directional theses require." This is the central defect: the artifact never actually measures anything about C's mechanism. There is no implied-correlation figure, no dispersion index, no options-skew or term-structure read, no single-name earnings-reaction magnitude — nothing that would let a reader distinguish "the fundamental case genuinely examined C's edge and found it broken" from "the shock/hawkish labels were already going to produce DO-NOT-ACTIVATE for every strategy this month, and C's paragraph explains why in C's vocabulary after the fact." Indeed the document says exactly that about the month as a whole: "every roster strategy is fundamentally DO-NOT-ACTIVATE this month, four of the five on raw reasoning and one (B) by override" — C is one of the four, and its reasoning is constructed from the same two axis values (`shock_overlay`, `policy_stance`) that also drive A, D and E to DO-NOT-ACTIVATE and that trip B's override.

The inferential chain from index-level style rotation to compressed catalyst dispersion is also a leap the document never earns. "growth −6.57%, small caps −3.08%, equal-weight +1.05%" is evidence of a duration/style factor dominating index-level returns; it is not evidence about correlation or dispersion in the cross-section that C actually trades (single-name event reactions), and a large style rotation is not incompatible with elevated single-name catalyst dispersion underneath it. The artifact asserts the compression rather than showing it.

Most damaging on its own terms: the same document, one paragraph earlier, builds Strategy B's raw case from exactly the surface-orderliness data — VIX, credit spreads, breadth, index level — that C's own question is most directly about, and C's paragraph does not cite any of it. That is selective use of the record's own evidence to reach a pre-determined DNA for C while using the identical calm data to argue "mechanism intact" for B (before B gets overridden on a different, universal rule). A reasoning process that reaches into the record for supportive evidence with respect to B but not C, when the two questions are nearly the same shape ("is the reaction/event environment still functioning"), is not evenhandedly applying M1a's scoring — it is telling a story that fits the pre-committed conclusion.

## Reasoning

The verdict follows from the artifact's own internal inconsistency, not from anything outside it: C's paragraph and B's paragraph sit one after another in the same document, ask closely related "is the reaction/event environment still functional" questions, and reach for entirely different evidentiary sets — B pulls the calm vol/credit/breadth data forward as its raw case, C never touches it. Combined with the circularity of resting C's DNA on a macro-shock label answering a macro-shock question, and the absence of any C-specific measurement anywhere in the reconciliation architecture (an admitted structural gap, not a hidden one), the reasoning does not clear the bar of a mechanism-specific finding — it reads as the roster's dominant macro narrative applied to C's vocabulary.

What I deliberately did NOT count against the fundamental claim: I did not treat the *absence* of a C-specific reconciliation rule, by itself, as proof the DNA call is wrong — Strategy.md may reasonably leave some strategies without mechanical override rules, and M1b's read-scope blinding (no macro_series access) is a legitimate architectural constraint on what evidence could even be cited, not a fabrication. I also did not attack the shock_overlay=acute scoring itself (Hormuz transit collapse, Brent +20.5%, sovereign spreads ~402bp) — those are measured, sourced figures in PART 1, not the weak link; the weak link is only the inferential jump from those figures to C's specific mechanism.

The strongest point AGAINST my own assigned position, stated honestly: macro-factor domination is a textbook, mechanically real threat to dispersion trading. If single-name returns are increasingly explained by a common discount-rate/oil factor rather than idiosyncratic catalysts, average pairwise correlation rises and dispersion compresses almost by definition — the growth/small-cap/equal-weight spread genuinely is consistent with a dominant common factor, and a central bank in a live, undecided hiking debate is a textbook cross-asset correlation driver. A reasonable adjudicator could conclude the *direction* of C's call is right even though the *reasoning offered for it in this document* is thin and selectively supported — that the conclusion happens to be defensible on priors the artifact didn't actually deploy. That is precisely why my verdict is about whether the fundamental CLAIM AS ARGUED survives adversarial attack, not about whether DO-NOT-ACTIVATE is the better trading decision in the abstract.
