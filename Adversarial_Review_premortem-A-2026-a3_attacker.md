## Reasoning

The task singles out one question: does the two-layer replacement (mechanical outer envelope + judgment-dependent inner budget) genuinely re-mitigate what the retired flat-2% cap covered, or does it re-label the same exposure as controlled? The artifact is unusually candid in places — it states outright that 2.13 is "flat (not reduced)" and that 2.18 is "STRENGTHENED" (worse) under the new regime, and it does not pretend the inner layer is bias-independent. That candor is a real strength and I did not manufacture defects against it.

But candor about *that* something is unmitigated does not substitute for the artifact being able to show *how it knows*. The single biggest problem is structural: the entire evidentiary basis for "flat" vs "STRENGTHENED" vs the un-quantified magnitude of the ordinal-tier effect is an unexplained token, "A2 2026," repeated three times as though self-evidently authoritative. Under the strict-blinding rule, if information necessary to the artifact's own claims is neither in the artifact nor in the trigger_context, that absence is itself the defect — I did not go looking for what A2 2026 might mean, and I should not have to. This alone would be sufficient for a Tier 1 verdict.

The second major defect is the broken pointer from the inner-layer self-reference concession to "see Known Limitations" — a promise the document's own revision header simultaneously admits is false (W4 remains open, unaddressed). This matters more than an ordinary citation slip because it is exactly the load-bearing admission the review was told to interrogate: whether the inner layer is attacked by anything other than the same judgment. The artifact's honest answer is embedded in the text ("subject to the same closure... not confronted further here") but the pointer to where that gap is supposedly tracked doesn't resolve, so a reader trusting the cross-reference would wrongly conclude the residual is documented and bounded when it isn't.

The third and fourth findings go to whether the sizing-mitigation labels correspond to real binding effects. Renaming "explicit probability" to "ordinal tier" is described as a mitigation for 2.13/2.26, but the document's own later admissions (judgment-dependent allocation; systematically larger recorded budgets from overconfident tiers) show it constrains representation, not outcome. Similarly, the one paragraph explicitly tasked with quantifying the residual reports only a worst-case delta (2%→10%, 5×) while neighboring text asserts the underlying bias is systematic, not tail-only — an internal mismatch in how severe the same revision believes its own residual to be.

I deliberately did NOT count as defects: (a) the prior cycle's Tier 2/3 residuals (W4's *label*, W5, W6, W7, the 2.19 buried-framing gap, the ~30%/~70% figure) — the trigger_context and the revision header both mark these explicitly out of scope for this cycle, and re-flagging them would violate the "not newly-introduced" instruction; (b) Constraint 2/3's self-reference architecture itself, which is stated as an accepted structural limitation across many prior cycles and is not something rev 8 touched; (c) the "STRENGTHENED"/"flat" qualitative vocabulary in isolation — the problem isn't the vocabulary, it's the absence of a sourced method behind it, which I folded into finding 1 rather than double-counting as a separate style complaint.
