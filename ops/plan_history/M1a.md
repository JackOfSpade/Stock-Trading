## H-M1a-03 — shock_overlay IS GRADED AGAINST A WRITTEN RUBRIC, NOT ON JUDGMENT ALONE (added 20260905, Rev 48; made symmetri

_Moved verbatim from `Claude_Task_Plan.md` (M1a, was line 3949) on 2026-10-30. Historical record only; the binding rule is the condensed text in the plan._

**`shock_overlay` IS GRADED AGAINST A WRITTEN RUBRIC, NOT ON JUDGMENT ALONE (added 2026-09-05, Rev 48; made symmetric by Rev 52, owner directive 2026-10-22, interactive session).** Score that axis per Strategy.md `## Regime scoring (strategy-blind, monthly)` → `### Shock / overlay grading rubric` (generated slice `strategy/09`), which fixes what counts as a qualifying shock event and a qualifying resolution event, the pre-shock Brent baseline and the shock elevation measured off it, the plateau re-base, the three legs and the three grades. Every transition, in either direction, uses ONE test and takes effect at the first scoring (this routine, or M1R out of cycle) whose measurement reads the other way; there is no dwell, quiet period or confirmation window in either direction. Apply it as follows. (a) **Structure.** The first qualifying event opens a shock structure, and a qualifying resolution event of that structure's channel closes it; narrative de-escalation with no measurable leg is not a qualifying resolution. (b) **Legs — each read on the scoring date alone, each with ONE threshold applied identically whichever grade the axis holds.** The price leg is ELEVATED when the shock elevation is greater than 0 AND the current Brent close is at or above baseline + 50% of the elevation (the midpoint). The sovereign-spread and FX legs are ACTIVE when measured AND attributed to the named channel per input category 4's thresholds; a leg that cannot be measured is NOT active. (c) **Plateau re-base — both halves required.** It fires when (1) all of Brent's trailing 10 trading-day closes lie within ±5% of their own median, read from the stored curated `BZUSD` series as recorded (a front-month roll gap is not adjusted), AND (2) no qualifying event is dated within those 10 sessions. This routine fires before the open, so the scoring session's own close does not exist yet: the 10 closes and the current close end at the latest completed session. When the re-base fires, the baseline becomes that 10-session median and the elevation is ZERO, so the price leg reads NOT ELEVATED until the next qualifying event; after that event the elevation is the peak close since it minus the re-based baseline. It applies whether the plateau sits above or below the prior baseline, and it is a re-measurement, not a grade change on its own. Half (2) is a claim about every one of the 10 sessions, so all of them must be swept for qualifying events (Section 4 covers the prior calendar month; sweep any session since its end the same way, and any earlier session item (e)(2)'s coverage reaches). (d) **Grades.** `acute` ⇔ a structure is open AND at least one leg is ELEVATED or ACTIVE; `latent` ⇔ a structure is open AND no leg is; `none` ⇔ no structure is open. A qualifying event while `none` opens a structure, and the grade then follows from the legs read on the scoring date: `acute` if any leg is ELEVATED or ACTIVE, otherwise `latent` (Rev 52, owner directive 2026-10-22, interactive session; review fix: the event qualifies on its own two-session move, while the legs are read on the scoring date). Rev 52 RETIRED the 15-trading-day event-recency leg of `acute`, the 15-quiet-day plus 50%-retrace downgrade requirement and the 60-quiet-day condition for `none`: apply none of them. (e) **Incomplete evidence holds the STANDING value, whatever it is, and only where it could change the grade (Rev 52, owner directive 2026-10-22, interactive session; review fix).** If this scoring cannot complete the event sweep a leg or a re-base needs, meaning that a session it could not sweep could hold an event that would change the grade, the axis keeps its standing value: it does not default to `acute`, and it does not default to lower. An unswept session that could not change the outcome holds nothing, so the hold is decided BY OUTCOME. (1) **Raise:** a qualifying event the sweep found (E), whose two-session window (THE PRICE MOVE OF A QUALIFYING EVENT IS READ OVER A TWO-SESSION WINDOW, below) has closed and meets the criterion, or whose closed sessions meet it on their own, establishes that a structure is OPEN (the axis is no longer `none`) even when sessions BEFORE it went unswept. That carve-out establishes only that a structure is open, never which session anchors it (review fix, Rev 52, owner directive 2026-10-22, interactive session). **ANCHOR CANDIDATES.** Where an unswept session before E (inside this sweep's coverage per (2), and after the re-base where one applies) could hold the structure's first qualifying event, the first qualifying event after a re-base, or a qualifying resolution event that closed the standing structure, evaluate the price leg off every candidate anchor it admits. For a re-based structure that no unswept resolution could have closed, E and the earliest unswept candidate session bound every case: first check whether the maximum `BZUSD` close since the earliest candidate equals the maximum since E, and if it does, proceed off E; otherwise evaluate both. From `none`, evaluate E and every candidate session, each with its own pre-shock baseline (the rubric's 60-session median before that anchor) and its own peak. **The FX leg's reference close follows the candidate (round-4 review fix, Rev 52, owner directive 2026-10-22, interactive session).** That close (THE FX LEG IS READ ON THE SCORING DATE AGAINST THE STRUCTURE'S REFERENCE CLOSE, below, rule (a)) is the close before the structure's first qualifying event under each reading. Where a candidate could change that event (from `none`, or where an unswept resolution could have closed the standing structure so that E opens a new one), evaluate the FX leg off each candidate's own reference close, alongside the price leg, in the every-reading comparison that follows. Where the structure's first qualifying event is on record and no unswept resolution could have closed it (a re-based structure included), read the FX leg once, off that recorded event. ## H-M1a-04 — THE PRICE MOVE OF A QUALIFYING EVENT IS READ OVER A TWOSESSION WINDOW FROM THE LAST CLOSE BEFORE ITS DATE (add

**THE PRICE MOVE OF A QUALIFYING EVENT IS READ OVER A TWO-SESSION WINDOW FROM THE LAST CLOSE BEFORE ITS DATE (added 2026-10-19, W5 SPEC-DEFECT NOTICE INTAKE adjudicated by an interactive owner session, closing W3's `shock_leg_i_qualifying_event_test_underspecified`, alert `11bbd3b7-d9f6-40e2-9101-6642c412d379`; this is the magnitude half of the clause whose dating half is the paragraph above).** The rubric's qualifying-event test borrows input category 4's "oil price move > $5/bbl" and "major currency > 2% move against USD" but states no window, so one development could pass or fail on the reader's choice. MEASURED on `state.signal_marks_curated` `BZUSD` for the 2026-09-23 development: last close before it 98.53, event session 103.08 (+4.55, fails), next session 106.60 (+8.07, passes). The currency leg has the same gap (the 2026-10-01 `shock_overlay` row read it "through 09-25" from an unstated start). **Rules**, binding on M1a/M1b/M1R scoring (the same set the paragraph above binds); a routine that measures a candidate without scoring the axis may measure its window the same way but does not thereby rule it qualifying: (a) **the window** — P is the last `BZUSD` close in `state.signal_marks_curated` strictly before the event date fixed by the paragraph above; S1 is the first `BZUSD` session on or after that date (a weekend or holiday event's S1 is the next session); S2 is the session after S1. The event's move is whichever of S1 − P and S2 − P goes further in the direction the development pushes the price, and the oil criterion is met only if it exceeds $5.00. A candidate qualifying resolution event is measured over the same window in the direction its resolution pushes the price (Rev 52, owner directive 2026-10-22, interactive session). The currency criterion uses the same three dates on a cited daily close of that currency against USD and is met only above 2%. **If S1 or S2 has not closed when the scoring runs, the window is INCOMPLETE** — routine, not rare: M1a fires at 11:00 UTC and M1R at 13:00 UTC (`ops/cadence.yaml`), both before that day's US close, so any development dated the prior session or the scoring day is in this state. Record which sessions have closed and their moves. **An INCOMPLETE window moves the axis in neither direction (Rev 52, owner directive 2026-10-22, interactive session; until Rev 52 it held the axis up under the retired "fast to raise, slow to lower" asymmetry).** A candidate — a qualifying shock event or a qualifying resolution event — whose closed sessions already meet the criterion on their own qualifies on them. Otherwise, a candidate that otherwise qualifies (a new dated event under rules (1) and (2) above, attributable to the named channel, and on the correct side of (d)) is recorded marked PENDING with the closed sessions' moves; where the grade turns on whether it qualifies — it would open or close a structure, fall inside the plateau re-base's 10 sessions, or restart a re-based elevation — the axis keeps its STANDING value, exactly as rule (3) above treats a doubtful date. The full window is read at the next scoring; do not count on M1R for it, because M1R fires only when D2a queues a refresh (its REFRESH REQUEST), never on a schedule. (b) **Why two sessions, not one or five** — the dating rule fixes the calendar date of first public report, not the hour, and a report landing after that day's close can only be priced the next session, so a one-session window would make qualification turn on a publication hour no surface records. A longer window admits drift as a shock: Brent rose 5.14 from 104.72 (2026-10-09) to 109.86 (2026-10-16) across five sessions whose largest single move was +3.94, so a five-session window would credit that accumulated drift to whatever development happened to be dated at its start. Two sessions is also the "a day or two" tolerance rule (3) above already uses. (c) **Two candidate dates** — where rule (3) above recorded two dates, compute the window off EACH and use the larger move; the date-dependent tests follow rule (3) above. A window off the later date alone starts after the re-pricing: for the Saudi pipeline strike, off 2026-09-10 P = 101.21 and S1 = 107.63 (+6.42, met), while off 2026-09-11 alone P = 107.63 and neither session closes above it. (d) **The window measures a candidate; it never creates one** — it applies only to a development that already counts as a new dated event under rules (1) and (2) above: a move with no new development is a re-pricing, and a consequence of a counted event does not become qualifying because its window clears the threshold. Each candidate is measured from its own P, so nothing that moved before its own date is credited to it, and attribution to the named channel (the rubric's clause (ii)) is still argued in the rationale. If a separately dated candidate falls on this candidate's S2, that session's move may qualify both only if attribution separates them; otherwise credit it to the later candidate. A move that relieves the channel — a ceasefire, a reopening, a flow recovery — is never a qualifying shock event however large (the 2026-10-01 `shock_overlay` row, `events.regime_events` `3b6d1923-0026-40b6-aad5-75f62eaa900c`, already applied this to recovering flows); since Rev 52 (owner directive 2026-10-22, interactive session) it is a candidate qualifying RESOLUTION event instead, measured by this same window in the direction of the relief, with its attribution to the channel's resolution argued in the rationale. (e) **A contract roll is not a move** — the stored `BZUSD` series is unadjusted continuous (the stored close fell 9.12 on 2026-09-29, the session it rolled from the November to the December contract; that same row records the roll itself as worth about −6.43, so most but not all of that print was the roll). If a roll falls between P and S2, read the window on one contract from a cited settlement source, or record the oil criterion UNMEASURED for that candidate. UNMEASURED is handled like an INCOMPLETE window under (a). Otherwise the candidate qualifies on another category-4 leg or not at all. This paragraph does not change how the price leg treats a roll, and the plateau re-base reads the stored series as recorded (the rubric's own rule).
