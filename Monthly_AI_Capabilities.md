Section 2 — AI trading performance research and reported results
Material items in March 2026:

FINSABER framework update — arXiv v5, 12 February 2026 (active through the March review window). Li, Kim, Cucuringu, and Ma's "Can LLM-based Financial Investing Strategies Outperform the Market in Long Run?" (arXiv 2505.07078) was revised on 12 February 2026 (v5) and was accepted to KDD 2026 in November 2025 GitHub [arXiv 2505.07078] [FINSABER GitHub]. Methodology: systematic backtests over two decades and 100+ symbols arXiv across multiple LLM strategies, with explicit controls for survivorship bias, look-ahead bias, and data-snooping bias. Models evaluated: GPT-family, multiple open-source LLM investors (specific Claude inclusion not highlighted in abstract). Results (adversarial to LLM-trading claims): "previously reported LLM advantages deteriorate significantly under broader cross-section and over a longer-term evaluation"; arXiv LLM strategies are "overly conservative in bull markets, underperforming passive benchmarks, and overly aggressive in bear markets, incurring heavy losses" arXiv [arXiv 2505.07078v5]. FLAG: the KDD-2026 acceptance and v5 revision occurred before March 2026 but the paper is now the field's primary adversarial-evidence artifact against LLM trading claims for the review-cycle context; no Claude-specific replication is highlighted.
"Toward Reliable Evaluation of LLM-Based Financial Multi-Agent Systems: Taxonomy, Coordination Primacy, and Cost Awareness" (arXiv 2603.27539). A March-2026 (arXiv ID 2603 prefix) survey covering 12 multi-agent trading systems and two single-agent baselines, introducing a four-dimensional taxonomy (architecture pattern, coordination mechanism, memory architecture, tool integration). The authors formulate the Coordination Primacy Hypothesis (CPH) — "inter-agent coordination protocol design is a primary driver of trading decision quality, often exerting greater influence than model scaling" — and present tiered structural evidence, with explicit warning against acting on published claims: "the gap between reported and robust performance is large enough to affect whether published claims should be acted upon" arxiv [arXiv 2603.27539v1]. The survey also flags systemic risk from correlated AI trading as an open concern: "If multiple institutions deploy similar LLM-based multi-agent architectures, correlated signals could amplify rather than dampen market volatility." Cross-provider; architectural generality claim.
"Calibrating Behavioral Parameters with Large Language Models" (arXiv 2602.01022). Evaluates four LLM variants — GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3.5 Haiku, and Gemini-2.5-Pro — across 19,200 agent–scenario pairs, eight canonical behavioral biases, in fully synthetic financial scenarios. Findings adversarial to "textbook-rational penalty" concern: "baseline LLM behavior exhibits systematic rationality bias: relative to human benchmarks, LLMs display near-zero disposition effects, somewhat attenuated herding relative to the 65-75% observed in cascade experiments, and low loss aversion (λ between 1.12 and 1.90 versus approximately 2.25)". Profile-based calibration induces large, stable, monotonic shifts (e.g., loss-averse profiles raise λ to 3.00; herding-prone profiles increase herding rates to 90%) [arXiv 2602.01022v1]. FLAG — Claude-family model evaluated (Claude 3.5 Haiku, older generation).

Section 3 — LLM failure-mode and bias research (March 2026)
Reverse chronological, March 2026 arXiv preprints and comparable primary sources:

"Know When You're Wrong: Aligning Confidence with Correctness for LLM Error Detection" (arXiv 2603.06604). Documents that reinforcement-learning-with-verifiable-rewards (RLVR) and DPO training paradigms "lead to miscalibration through reward exploitation", while supervised fine-tuning (SFT) "produces well-calibrated confidence through maximum-likelihood estimation". Baseline Qwen3-4B-Instruct exhibits ECE 0.163; post-RL SFT recovery brings it to 0.034 [arXiv 2603.06604]. Architectural generality claim — the paper argues miscalibration "consistently manifests as distribution sharpening across various models" and identifies the RL stage as the causal culprit. FLAG — claims architectural generality across autoregressive LLMs trained with modern RLHF/RLVR. This directly updates the existing "probabilistic miscalibration" disadvantage (2.13): the finding is not merely that LLMs are miscalibrated, but that current RL-based post-training actively degrades calibration.
"The Dunning–Kruger Effect in Large Language Models: An Empirical Study of Confidence Calibration" (arXiv 2603.09985, dated 12 February 2026 but in the March arXiv batch). Evaluates four LLMs — including Claude Haiku 4.5 — across four benchmarks, 24,000 trials. Kimi K2 at 23.3% accuracy exhibits 95.7% mean confidence (ECE 0.726). Claude Haiku 4.5 achieves the best calibration (ECE 0.122) with the highest confidence variability (std 41.0) and uniquely displays underconfidence on HellaSwag (overconfidence score –0.089), "the only such case observed across all model-dataset combinations" [arXiv 2603.09985]. FLAG — Claude-family model evaluated. Update adversarial to the miscalibration disadvantage (2.13) in the Claude-specific direction: at least one current Claude variant appears measurably better-calibrated than most peers in this benchmark — but Haiku 4.5 is not the workflow decision-maker, and the benchmark is general QA, not financial probabilistic reasoning.
"Balancing Classification and Calibration Performance in Decision-Making LLMs via Calibration Aware Reinforcement Learning" (arXiv 2601.13284). Demonstrates that RLVR "produces extremely overconfident models, whereas SFT yields substantially better calibration, even under distribution shift, though with smaller performance gains" and that decision tokens "inherit overconfidence from reasoning traces and that reinforcement learning cannot achieve calibration, since there are no calibrated paths to reinforce from the base model" [arXiv 2601.13284]. Architectural generality claim — the finding is framed as a structural property of how RL post-training interacts with decision-token probability.
"Calibration Across Layers: Understanding Calibration Evolution in LLMs" (arXiv 2511.00280, November 2025, actively cited in the March literature). Identifies a "distinct confidence correction phase in the upper/later layers" of transformer-based LLMs and a low-dimensional calibration direction in the residual stream whose perturbation improves ECE/MCE without harming accuracy [arXiv 2511.00280]. Architectural generality claim.
"TradeTrap: Are LLM-based Trading Agents Truly Reliable and Faithful?" (arXiv 2512.02261, December 2025, cited heavily in the March 2026 corpus). Systematic stress-testing of LLM trading agents demonstrates an " arXivepistemic hallucination" failure mode: under adversarial data injection the agent "erroneously believ[ed] it still retain[ed] the position it had fully liquidated the previous day … strategic paralysis, where the agent bases its decision-making on a phantom portfolio, effectively decoupling its internal reasoning from the ground truth of its execution history" [arXiv 2512.02261]. Architectural generality: the paper asserts this is a property of LLM-based trading agents generally under tool-mediated execution. Also demonstrates adversarial data injection produces severe strategic incoherence (aggressive accumulation during manufactured crashes, total liquidation on recoveries).
"Adversarial News and Lost Profits: Manipulating Headlines in LLM-Driven Algorithmic Trading" (arXiv 2601.13082, March 2026 period). Evaluates Unicode homoglyph substitutions and hidden-text-clause injection against an LSTM+LLM-sentiment algorithmic trading system (FinBERT, FinGPT, FinLLaMA, and six general-purpose LLMs). Result: a one-day attack sustained over 14 months "can reliably mislead LLMs and reduce annual returns by up to 17.7 percentage points" [arXiv 2601.13082v1]. Models evaluated include general-purpose LLMs in addition to finance-specific models; specific Claude inclusion not explicitly confirmed in the excerpt. Architectural generality: homoglyph and hidden-text vulnerabilities are documented architectural properties of tokenizer-based NLP pipelines.
"AlphaForgeBench" (arXiv 2602.18481), cited in March 2026 literature. Documents under deterministic decoding (temperature = 0) that "LLMs generate completely different trading action sequences across runs on identical market data, exhibiting no consistency in decision-making patterns" and "rapid action flipping persists despite hard constraints" [arXiv 2602.18481]. Architectural generality claim — directly updates the "cross-session inconsistency" (2.24) and "within-session consistency" (1.2) items. Models evaluated include mainstream LLMs; Claude-specific inclusion is implicit across the benchmark but not called out.
International AI Safety Report 2026 (cited March 2026 update) — found sophisticated attackers bypass best-defended models ~50% of the time with just 10 prompt-injection attempts; Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6 System Card quantifies a single prompt-injection attempt against a GUI-based agent succeeding 17.8% of the time without safeguards [Blog summary citing Claude Opus 4.6 System Card, updated March 2026]. FLAG — Claude-family model quantified. Anthropic reports that Opus 4.5 browser agent reduced attack success to ~1% via RL and classifier defenses.

Summary for Section 3: Two independent streams of March-2026 research — (a) RL-post-training causing systematic miscalibration as a structural property and (b) TradeTrap-style demonstrations of epistemic hallucination and strategic paralysis in LLM trading agents — provide adversarial-generality evidence against edge claims 1.5, 1.7, 1.8 and disadvantage claims 2.3, 2.13, 2.24. The one counter-signal for Claude specifically is Haiku 4.5's best-in-class calibration in the Dunning-Kruger paper — but Haiku 4.5 is not the workflow model.
