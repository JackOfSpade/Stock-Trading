## 1. SPY Trend Character

**Total return.** The S&P 500 index finished Q1 2026 with a total-return loss of approximately **−4.3%** on a market-cap-weighted basis, and a price decline of **−4.6%** — "the worst quarterly period since the third quarter of 2022" ([Motley Fool / Globe and Mail, Apr 10, 2026](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/stocks/INTC/pressreleases/1243875/3-key-takeaways-from-the-sp-500s-worst-performing-quarter-since-2022/); [Landmark Wealth Management, Q1 2026 review](https://landmarkwealthmgmt.com/articles/sp-500-q1-2026-why-your-diversified-portfolio-may-have-beaten-the-index/)). The S&P 500 equal-weight index was roughly flat to slightly positive (+0.7%), highlighting that the drawdown was concentrated in mega-caps ([Landmark Wealth Management](https://landmarkwealthmgmt.com/articles/sp-500-q1-2026-why-your-diversified-portfolio-may-have-beaten-the-index/)). A specific SPY-ETF total-return number for the calendar quarter was **not retrievable from public sources within the research timeframe** at identical precision, but SPY tracks SPX closely and — per State Street's official SPY performance page reporting "as of Mar 31 2026" — moved in line with the index ([State Street SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust product page](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/etfs/spdr-sp-500-etf-trust-spy)).

**Path description.** The quarter took a **rolling top followed by a sharp, geopolitically-driven drawdown** shape. The S&P 500 made new record intraday and closing highs in January — the index closed at a record 6,978.60 on January 26/27 ([CNBC, Jan 26, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/26/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)) — then drifted lower through February, and fell sharply after the February 28 outbreak of the US–Israel–Iran war. Advisor Perspectives' weekly snapshots confirm the January 27, 2026 all-time high and note the index had fallen "3.42% off its all-time high from January 27, 2026" by early March and was "below the 50-day moving average since February 27th" ([dshort/Advisor Perspectives, Mar 6, 2026](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/03/06/s-p-500-snapshot-lowest-close-of-2026)). The path was therefore best described as: January grind higher to record, February rolling top, late-February through late-March sharp downtrend driven by the Iran war shock, with stabilization in the final two sessions of the quarter (March 30–31).

**50-day / 200-day SMA relationship.** Per Advisor Perspectives, "the 50-day moving average has been above the 200-day moving average since July 1st" [2025] and remained so through the January 27, 2026 high; no golden cross or death cross on the SPX 50/200 SMA was reported during Q1 2026 ([dshort/Advisor Perspectives, Mar 6, 2026](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/03/06/s-p-500-snapshot-lowest-close-of-2026)). The index itself fell below its 50-day SMA on/around February 27 and remained below it for much of March, but the 50>200 SMA relationship itself was not broken at the index level within the quarter.

**Major intra-quarter moves (≥3%).**
- **Jan 1–Jan 27 rally:** S&P 500 rose from ~6,966 (Jan 2) to a record 6,978.60 close on Jan 26/27, then 6,944 intraweek low ([CNBC, Jan 26, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/26/stock-market-today-live-updates.html); [dshort/Advisor Perspectives, Jan 2, 2026](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/01/02/s-p-500-snapshot-index-posts-16-4-gain-in-2025)).
- **Feb 3–4 software-driven tech selloff:** Nasdaq-heavy session driven by "Claude Cowork" launch fears; software ETF IGV fell below $85 intraday on Feb 3, a ~28% drawdown from its September 2025 peak ([StockCharts, Feb 4, 2026](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/the-claude-crash-how-ai-triggered-a-historic-selloff-in-software-stocks/)).
- **Feb 28 – Mar 9 war shock:** The US–Israel strike on Iran the weekend of Feb 28 triggered the largest drawdown of the quarter. The Dow fell over 400 points (–0.7% on S&P) on Mar 2 and extended losses into mid-March ([Wikipedia, Economic impact of the 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_the_2026_Iran_war); [NPR, Mar 2, 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/02/nx-s1-5732287/iran-war-oil-gasoline-prices)).
- **Late-March stabilization and brief relief rally on Mar 27** — the day the VIX peaked (see Section 2).

**Quarter-start / end / high / low.** Quarter-start: S&P 500 closed at 6,966 on the final trading day of 2025, making Jan 2 open price near that level ([Wall Street Horizon/IBKR, Jan 12, 2026](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/macro/q4-2025-earnings-preview-can-corporate-profits-sustain-sp-500-record-highs/)). Quarter-end: the March 31 S&P 500 close is consistent with a −4.6% price decline from the 2025 close of 6,966, implying a close around **~6,646** at quarter-end; the exact Mar 31, 2026 closing tick was not retrievable from a primary source in the research window, but April 1 opening reporting shows the index at 6,575.32 after a +0.72% gain ([CNBC, Mar 31/Apr 1, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/31/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)), consistent with a Mar 31 close of roughly **6,528** (the CNBC figure is the Apr 1 close); **the precise Mar 31, 2026 S&P 500 closing value is not retrievable from public sources within the research timeframe**, but third-party summaries converge on a quarterly price decline of −4.6%. Quarter high: **6,978.60** (closing) on **January 26, 2026** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/26/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)); intraday record was set the following session. Quarter low was set in late March during the Iran war; **the precise Q1 2026 SPX intraday low with date is not retrievable from the sources reviewed within the research timeframe**.

## 2. Volatility Regime

**VIX range and averages.** The VIX closed at **19.86** on the last trading day before the Iran war began (Friday Feb 27, 2026) ([CoinDesk, Apr 7, 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/07/bitcoin-pulls-away-from-software-stocks-as-iran-war-ai-reshape-market-dynamic) — citing contemporaneous trading data). The VIX reached an **intraday high of 35.3 on March 9, 2026** ([The People's Economist, citing CBOE/Bloomberg](https://thepeopleseconomist.substack.com/p/tracking-vix-index-volatility-reaction)), with a secondary closing-basis peak on **March 27 at 31.05 (intraday high 31.65)** ([CNBC, Apr 7, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/07/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). Low for the quarter was in mid-January, with VIX trading well below 20 as the SPX set record highs; the lowest daily close of the quarter is **not retrievable from public sources within the research timeframe** with an exact date, but early-January readings were well below the 20 threshold per the LPL commentary that described the VIX "stair-stepping higher since the end of last year" ([LPL Research weekly commentary, early Mar 2026](https://www.lpl.com/research/weekly-market-commentary/markets-tested-as-iran-conflict-continues.html)). Primary source: [FRED VIXCLS series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VIXCLS) contains the authoritative daily history. **Quarterly average VIX is not retrievable from public sources within the research timeframe** without direct database access.

**Realized S&P 500 volatility for Q1 2026 (annualized).** **Data not retrievable from public sources within the research timeframe** as a single published number; however, the CBOE/LSEG commentary around the Iran conflict implies realized volatility materially above the ~10–12% level that had prevailed in late 2025.

**Volatility events.**
- **Early February software selloff:** The Feb 3 session saw IGV record its largest single-day ETF volume in its 25-year history; yet, as Fullerton Fund Management noted, "US equity market volatility has not been large enough – the 'fear' index (i.e. the VIX) has been well contained" at that stage ([Fullerton Fund Management, Feb 2026](https://www.fullertonfund.com/fullerton-insights/us-software-sell-off-and-rotational-plays-february-2026/)). The VIX did not reach 25 during the software washout alone.
- **VIX >25 episode (Mar 2 – late March):** From the opening of trade on Mar 2, 2026 (first session after the Feb 28 US–Israel strike on Iran) the VIX moved sustainedly above 25, peaking at an intraday **35.3 on March 9** and later closing at **31.05 on March 27** ([The People's Economist](https://thepeopleseconomist.substack.com/p/tracking-vix-index-volatility-reaction); [CNBC Apr 7, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/07/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). LPL described the quarter's VIX pattern as "stair-stepping higher" throughout Q1 ([LPL Research](https://www.lpl.com/research/weekly-market-commentary/markets-tested-as-iran-conflict-continues.html)).
- **No sustained sub-15 compressions** were observed during Q1 2026, per the FRED/CBOE record and contemporaneous commentary that noted the VIX "stair-stepped higher since the end of last year" ([StockCharts via Mike Zaccardi, Apr 2026](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/risk-on-ignites-what-us-iran-truce-means-for-stock-markets/)). The VIX closed at **19.86 on Feb 27** and was already drifting upward from January lows ([CoinDesk, Apr 7, 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/07/bitcoin-pulls-away-from-software-stocks-as-iran-war-ai-reshape-market-dynamic)).

**Comparison to late-2025 baseline.** Late-2025 was a benign-volatility environment with SPX making record highs into year-end at 6,966 ([Wall Street Horizon/IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/macro/q4-2025-earnings-preview-can-corporate-profits-sustain-sp-500-record-highs/)), and VIX persistently sub-20. Q1 2026 saw a clear regime shift from NORMAL-to-LOW readings in January toward sustained HIGH (>25) readings from early-March through late-March, with the war serving as the regime trigger.

## 3. Yield Curve Trajectory

**10-year Treasury yield.** The 10-year yield **"rose from approximately 4.19% at the start of the year to 4.34% by quarter-end"** ([Cerity Partners Q1 2026 Review](https://ceritypartners.com/insights/q1-2026-review-and-q2-2026-economic-and-market-outlook/)). The **intraweek low was around 3.97% in late February**, just before the war broke out, per CNBC's April 22 narrative: "The yield was at just 3.97% in late February, before the war with Iran broke out" ([PBS News, Apr 2026](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/oil-prices-rise-and-stocks-give-back-part-of-record-breaking-rally-following-latest-iran-tensions)). The quarterly high on a weekly-average basis was reported at **4.04% at end-February** per Advisor Perspectives' long-term yield piece ([Advisor Perspectives, Mar 2, 2026](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/03/02/10-year-treasury-yield-long-term-perspective-february-2026)); however, the 10-year rose further in March as the war pushed oil prices higher, with the quarter-end value of ~4.34% representing the Q1 high. Primary source for daily history: [FRED DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10).

**2-year Treasury yield.** Per Wolf Street's yield-curve analysis: "The 1-year Treasury yield shot up by 33 basis points since the beginning of March, removing the rate cut from its less-than-one-year window, and pricing in the potential for a rate hike by late 2026. This spike in yields started at the beginning of March after the war in Iran kicked off" ([Wolf Street, Mar 21, 2026](https://wolfstreet.com/2026/03/21/2-year-3-year-treasury-yields-spike-flip-to-rate-hike-yield-curve-uninverts-government-sold-606-billion-of-treasury-securities-this-week-as-the-borrowing-must-go-on/)). Exact 2-year daily closes for Jan 2 and Mar 31, 2026 are **not retrievable from public sources within the research timeframe** without direct FRED download, but the series is authoritatively published at [FRED DGS2](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS2) and the H.15 release ([Federal Reserve H.15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/)). The direction is clear: 2-year yields rose materially over March.

**Yield curve inversion state.** The 10Y–2Y curve was **not inverted** at either end of Q1 2026. Wolf Street explicitly described the curve as having **"solidly uninverted"** by late March, with "the sag in the middle" being eliminated as 1-to-5-year yields rose the most on the war-inflation repricing ([Wolf Street, Mar 21, 2026](https://wolfstreet.com/2026/03/21/2-year-3-year-treasury-yields-spike-flip-to-rate-hike-yield-curve-uninverts-government-sold-606-billion-of-treasury-securities-this-week-as-the-borrowing-must-go-on/)). The FRED T10Y2Y spread series confirms this was positive territory in Q1 2026 ([FRED T10Y2Y](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/T10Y2Y)). There was no inversion→normalization transition during Q1 2026 because the curve was already normal entering the quarter; the SUSTAINED flag did not apply.

**Notable yield moves tied to macro events.**
- **January 28, 2026 FOMC:** Fed held federal funds target at 3.50%–3.75%; two dissents preferred a cut ([Federal Reserve FOMC Minutes, Jan 27–28, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20260128.htm); [J.P. Morgan Wealth Management](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/markets-and-economy/economy/fed-meeting-january-2026)). The decision was largely priced in and produced limited yield action.
- **Feb 28 – early March:** War onset drove a **steepening** of the curve as inflation expectations repriced; 10-year rose from ~3.97% to above 4.30% by late March ([Wolf Street](https://wolfstreet.com/2026/03/21/2-year-3-year-treasury-yields-spike-flip-to-rate-hike-yield-curve-uninverts-government-sold-606-billion-of-treasury-securities-this-week-as-the-borrowing-must-go-on/); [PBS News](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/oil-prices-rise-and-stocks-give-back-part-of-record-breaking-rally-following-latest-iran-tensions)).
- **March 18, 2026 FOMC:** Fed held rates again; Summary of Economic Projections raised the 2026 PCE inflation forecast to 2.7% (from 2.5% in December), kept the dot-plot at one 2026 cut and one 2027 cut, and raised the long-run neutral rate to 3.1%. Governor Miran dissented in favor of a cut ([Federal Reserve FOMC statement, Mar 18, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260318a.htm); [CNBC, Mar 18, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/18/fed-interest-rate-decision-march-2026.html); [Kiplinger Mar 18 live](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/live/march-fed-meeting-2026-live-updates-and-commentary)).
- **Inflation prints:** December 2025 CPI year-over-year came in at 2.7% ([J.P. Morgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/markets-and-economy/economy/fed-meeting-january-2026)); February 2026 CPI at 2.4% YoY vs. January's 2.7%, with January PCE "the Fed's preferred measure of inflation" running "at the worst in nearly two years" at 3.1% core ([Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/live/march-fed-meeting-2026-live-updates-and-commentary); [Wolf Street](https://wolfstreet.com/2026/03/21/2-year-3-year-treasury-yields-spike-flip-to-rate-hike-yield-curve-uninverts-government-sold-606-billion-of-treasury-securities-this-week-as-the-borrowing-must-go-on/)).

**Prior inversion duration.** The 10Y–2Y curve had uninverted prior to Q1 2026 (positive by mid-late 2024), so no SUSTAINED-inversion carry-in applied entering the quarter; the Q1 2026 regime was NORMAL throughout.

**Percentage of S&P 500 constituents above 200-day SMA.** Primary reference: [S&P Global / MacroMicro S5TH series](https://en.macromicro.me/series/22718/sp-500-200ma-breadth). **Exact Jan 2 and Mar 31, 2026 percentages are not retrievable with numerical precision from public sources within the research timeframe**; the MacroMicro series confirms recent readings in the high-50s/low-60s by April 20, 2026 (59–60%). Qualitatively, the trajectory was as follows:

- Breadth deteriorated sharply in March during the Iran war and tech drawdown. Based on the S&P Global Sector Dashboard "Energy had the highest relative strength among large-cap sectors" at quarter-end, with Technology, Consumer Discretionary, and Communication Services sectors declining ([S&P Dow Jones U.S. Sector Dashboard, Mar 31, 2026](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/performance-reports/dashboard-us-sector.pdf)).

This evidence is inconsistent with a purely narrow/mega-cap-led tape — the median stock held up far better than the index. The pattern was thus a **mega-cap-led drawdown with broad rotation** into Energy, Materials, Utilities, Staples, Real Estate, and Health Care ([Benzinga/Yardeni, Feb 2026](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/market-summary/26/02/50639291/ai-selloff-software-slump-sector-rotation-yardeni-2026)).

## 5. Dominant Macro Narrative

**Dominant themes.** Q1 2026 was dominated by **two intertwining themes** that shifted in sequence:

1. **"SaaSpocalypse" / AI disruption of software** — the quarter-start dominant narrative. Enterprise software names were repriced on fear that AI agents (Anthropic's Claude Cowork, OpenAI's Project Operator) would cannibalize per-seat SaaS pricing. Bloomberg reporting synthesized: "Panic over a software-business disruption from AI appears to have subsided [only] after Anthropic showcased its partnership with Salesforce" in later April ([StockAnalysis / CNBC excerpts](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/igv/)); the quarter witnessed >$2 trillion in software market capitalization wiped out ([Humai, citing Bulloak Capital analysis](https://www.humai.blog/saaspocalypse-why-enterprise-software-has-lost-more-than-2-trillion-in-2026/)).
2. **US–Israel–Iran war and oil-driven inflation shock** — the quarter-end dominant narrative, which took over from Feb 28 onward. Brent crude surged from ~$72/bbl on Feb 27 to nearly $120/bbl at its peak ([CNBC, Apr 21, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/21/oil-price-iran-war-middle-east.html)), and WTI crossed $100/bbl for the first time since 2022 ([FactSet Energy, citing Nathan Hasbrook](https://insight.factset.com/sp-500-energy-and-utilities-sectors-earnings-previews-q1-2026)). Brent rose 51% in March alone ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/21/oil-price-iran-war-middle-east.html)).

**Key inflection points.**
- **Jan 26–27, 2026:** SPX record close at 6,978.60 — the peak of the 2025-carry-in momentum narrative ([CNBC, Jan 26, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/26/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)).
- **Jan 28, 2026 FOMC:** Fed held rates unchanged; two dissents; narrative slightly hawkish ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20260128.htm)).

## 7. Overall Regime Characterization

Q1 2026 was a **transitional, macro-driven, risk-off-ending quarter with pronounced sector rotation**. The quarter began as a trending/risk-on continuation of 2025 — the S&P 500 set an all-time closing high on January 27 at 6,978.60 amid a generally HEALTHY breadth backdrop and a NORMAL (>15, <25) VIX regime — but shifted decisively through two successive shocks: the February software/AI-positioning washout ("Claude Crash"/SaaSpocalypse, IGV down ~24%+ in Q1) and then, from February 28, the US–Israel–Iran war, which pushed crude +55% and the VIX into the HIGH regime (peak 35.3 intraday on March 9) through March. The resulting tape was **idiosyncratic at the sector level** — Energy +37%, Materials +9.3%, Utilities +7.5% while Technology −9.3% — producing a **mega-cap-led headline drawdown (−4.6% SPX) with a surprisingly broad underlying cross-section (equal-weight SPX +0.7%, 290/500 names beat the index)**, best classified as **rotation rather than a pure risk-off collapse**. Entering the quarter the SPY trend state was UP (close > 50SMA > 200SMA) with HEALTHY breadth and LOW-NORMAL VIX; by quarter-end the trend state had weakened to NEUTRAL (price below 50SMA since Feb 27, though 50SMA>200SMA preserved), VIX regime was HIGH, breadth had rolled over from its Feb 27 peak into weaker-but-above-50% territory, and the yield curve remained NORMAL throughout (no SUSTAINED inversion carry-in). The net characterization: **transitional, macro/geopolitical-driven, range-ended-lower, rotationally idiosyncratic** — not a monolithic risk-off event, but a regime shift triggered by two distinct narrative shocks (AI-disruption repricing of software, then energy/geopolitical inflation shock) layered onto a still-growing earnings backdrop (Q4 2025 blended EPS +14.2% YoY; Q1 2026 forward estimates revised modestly lower but still tracking >13% YoY).
