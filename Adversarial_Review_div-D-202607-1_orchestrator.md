## (d) Verdict reasoning

Strategy D's fundamental question is whether structural headwinds argue against *initiating new* long-horizon positions this month. Decomposing the three legs rather than treating them as fungible: the growth leg is genuinely noisy (two reversals in three readings) and the shock leg, while real, is not architecturally assigned to D by any reconciliation rule and is shown by the artifact's own text to be already partially unwinding. That alone might leave me at "weak DNA, low confidence." What moves me to a confident DO-NOT-ACTIVATE is the reconstructed June precedent: the June orchestrator's actual (recovered) reasoning rested on the discount-rate regime being *unchanged* since the review that lifted the block — an explicit consistency test, not merely "the override was mechanical." That regime has now moved, and moved in the hawkish direction, not stayed flat. Applying the prior orchestrator's own stated test consistently argues for DNA on a basis independent of the override-manufactured question altogether. The inflation-axis knife-edge finding cuts the other way in one respect — it means the "raw vs. override-manufactured" distinction this review leans on is itself fragile and likely transient — but that observation discounts the *strength* of the reason to reconsider DNA, it doesn't supply a reason to activate.

Default-on-ambiguity (Strategy.md:158-160) is not what is driving this verdict — I reach DO-NOT-ACTIVATE affirmatively, on the merits of the recovered June-consistency argument and the policy-stance leg, not because I am unable to decide.
