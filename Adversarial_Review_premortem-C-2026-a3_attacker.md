### W5 — Tier 1 — Replacing the fixed 2% cap with an AI-set, thesis-scaled budget is a weaker control precisely against the disadvantage it defends against, and the artifact's reasoning never engages this
**Anchor:** "2.18 (instruction adherence over capital preservation..." (08_pre_mortems.md:557, title); "Defined-risk structures cap maximum loss at a pre-committed premium, which is the structural compensation for AI_Edges 2.18" (08_pre_mortems.md:479)

2.18 is defined by the artifact itself as the risk that the AI follows instructions/conviction over preserving capital. The load-bearing property of a *fixed* 2% cap against that specific risk is that it is exogenous to Claude's judgment at trade time — no amount of instruction-following bias can inflate a number Claude does not set. Thesis-scaled CaR budgeting (per trigger context) makes the per-thesis allocation itself a function of Claude's assessed thesis conviction, up to a 10%-per-name ceiling — i.e., sizing becomes an output of the very cognitive process 2.18 names as biased toward capital-unsafe instruction-following. The artifact's framing that "the defined-risk structure and the 2% cap are the same mechanism viewed from two angles" (line 557) treats the cap as inert/structural and never considers that under a budgeting regime where the cap-equivalent is AI-set rather than fixed, the cap-equivalent reintroduces exactly the discretion that made it a compensating control for 2.18 in the first place. This is not merely a magnitude change (2% vs. up to 10%) — it is a category change from "hard external constraint" to "AI-set allocation," and the artifact offers zero replacement reasoning for why 2.18 remains adequately compensated once that category changes at the same time A1 found 2.18 itself strengthened.
