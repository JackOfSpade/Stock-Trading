## 2.24 — favourable and unfavourable halves, weighed separately

These are genuine instances of the favourable pattern trigger_context describes (design vs. deterministic execution), and the text does not overclaim them — it explicitly scopes component (a) to the "divergence-existence layer" only (978) and treats component (b)'s narrative-validity layer as unmitigated by the same mechanism (979). This half is sufficient as written; no complaint manufactured here.

### Unfavourable (memory-mediated contamination)
This half is where the artifact fails. Every reference to cross-session convergence risk in this section attributes it exclusively to weight-level bias: "same-weight-level-bias-cuts-across-sessions property" (961), "different sessions could converge on similarly-biased threshold choices due to weight-level bias" (979), "weight-level bias on the narrative's *choice* of which quantitative threshold to commit to remains exposed" (981), and KL#16's review trigger treats any observed clustering of `reconvergence_thresholds` values across pairs purely as "evidence of threshold-choice convergence (a 2.24 manifestation)" (1050) — i.e., as proof of shared-weight bias.

But this artifact's own Section 6 logging architecture (999–1017) creates exactly the channel trigger_context warns about: `Decision_Log.md` persists each pair's `reconvergence_thresholds`, `percentile_at_entry`, `spread_definition`, and `anchor_2_reference_set_size` (1001–1007) — durable, session-external artifacts that a *later* thesis-construction session plausibly reads (the pair-prioritization rule at 889 already establishes that later sessions operate "across the full candidate pool at the start of each thesis-construction session," i.e., sessions are not blind to accumulated strategy state). If a later session's threshold choice is steered by having read a prior session's persisted 5%-vs-3% choice — anchoring, not independent weight-level convergence, and occurring "even when the model refuses the harmful action" of copying it outright — KL#16's audit (1050) would record the identical observable signature (clustering of threshold values) and misattribute it to weight-level bias every time, because the artifact's own causal vocabulary has no other category. This is not a cosmetic gap: the two channels call for different fixes (weight-level bias is treated here as an irreducible structural residual; memory-mediated contamination is fixable by restricting what a later thesis-construction session can read from prior `Decision_Log.md` entries). The artifact's mitigation text — Constraint 3 (977–982) and KL#16 (1050) — nowhere distinguishes them, and E is named in the trigger_context as the heaviest 2.24 consumer of the two (x42), making this the strategy where the conflation matters most. Per the task's own criterion, this is a Tier 1 defect (W8).
