# Regime Condition Axis Scoring

## growth_momentum: decelerating

The Q1 2026 GDP second estimate (released May 28) revised the headline rate DOWN to +1.6% annualized from the advance reading of +2.0%, with downward revisions concentrated in private inventory investment and services-side consumer spending — a material softening of the rebound off the shutdown-affected Q4 2025 (+0.5%). April retail sales (released May 15) decelerated to +0.5% MoM from March's revised +1.6%, and the ISM Services New Orders sub-index fell 7.1 percentage points to 53.5 while the headline Services PMI eased to 53.6. Forward sentiment cratered: the University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment Index printed a record-low 44.8 (third consecutive monthly decline), and the Conference Board CCI eased to 93.1; reported S&P 500 Q1 earnings strength (+28.6% blended growth, 84% beat rate) reflects the prior quarter's activity and does not contradict the directional softening in real-time activity data and consumer expectations.

## inflation_trend: reaccelerating

The April CPI release (May 13) printed +3.8% headline YoY / +0.6% MoM SA with core at +2.8% YoY / +0.4% MoM SA — the largest core monthly print since January 2025 and roughly double the February and March pace, with the acceleration concentrated in services (airfare +2.8% MoM, +20.7% YoY) and energy (+3.8% MoM after March's record gasoline print) while goods were flat MoM. April PPI Final Demand reached +6.0% YoY (+1.4% MoM SA) — the largest 12-month advance since +6.4% in December 2022, accelerating sharply from March's already-elevated +4.0% YoY. Inflation expectations are reinforcing: 1-year UMich expectations rose to 4.8% and 5–10 year expectations to 3.9%, and economists cited in release commentary anticipated CPI could surpass 4% in the May print with core near 3%. The recent Brent decline (~–19% MoM to ~$93) is a forward disinflationary signal not yet present in May's release data.

## policy_stance: hawkish

The April 28–29 FOMC minutes (released May 21) documented a broad hawkish drift: "many participants" indicated preference for removing the easing-bias language from the post-meeting statement, and a majority noted "some policy tightening may be needed were inflation to continue running persistently above the Committee's 2% target." The 8–4 April vote remained the largest dissent count since 1992, with three of the four dissents specifically against retaining easing-bias language. Chair-level transition tilts incrementally hawkish at the margin: Kevin Warsh was Senate-confirmed May 13 (54–45, a record-narrow margin) and sworn in May 22 with framing centered on bringing inflation down without trading off growth, and the federal-funds-path commentary repriced "later and shallower" cuts through the month. No FOMC meeting occurred in May; the target range remained 3.50–3.75%.

## risk_sentiment: risk-on

Risk-asset signals are unambiguous: the S&P 500 set fresh all-time highs (May 1 close 7,230; May 29 close 7,580; ~+4.25% monthly return), investment-grade corporate OAS held ~77–80 bps near 25-year tights with high-yield OAS ~280–320 bps also tight, and the VIX continued decompressing into the low-to-mid teens (May 29 close 15.32). Cross-asset signals reinforced rather than offset: Brent fell ~19% MoM on ceasefire-progress newsflow, the DXY drifted ~+1% MoM range-bound, and the 10-year Treasury yield held a moderate ~4.45% with the curve modestly steepening. Two breadth-quality caveats are noted but do not redirect the call: only ~54% of S&P 500 constituents traded above their 50-day moving average (moderate participation) and University of Michigan consumer sentiment fell to a record low — material forward-quality concerns but not yet expressed in risk-asset pricing or in credit-spread behavior. "Stressed" is excluded by the credit-spread and equity evidence; the asset-pricing read in May moves one step beyond April's "neutral" toward risk-on.

## shock_overlay: latent

The Iran war remained active through May (~3 months running) and the Strait of Hormuz commercial traffic stayed near ~4% of pre-crisis volume as of May 24 (4 vessels vs typical ~95/day; >1,550 vessels stranded by May 8), but the kinetic phase did not escalate further: the U.S.–Iran 60-day MOU and ceasefire-extension talks advanced through month-end, the May 4–5 oil spike on ceasefire-wobble was reversed by month-end, and Brent fell to ~$93 (still elevated vs the ~$70s pre-war baseline but well off the early-April ~$128 peak). A secondary domestic shock layer continued via tariff legal uncertainty (May 7 U.S. Court of International Trade ruling against Section 122 tariffs limited to three plaintiffs; May 12 Federal Circuit administrative stay), but the net practical impact on the effective tariff rate (~11.8% per Yale Budget Lab) is muted while appeal proceeds. "None" is excluded because active real-economy transmission (Hormuz shipping at ~4% of normal; tariff-driven cost pressure still working through prices) persists; "acute" is excluded because the kinetic phase paused, the diplomatic track materially advanced, and equity/credit/oil markets all moved in shock-recovery direction through the month.
