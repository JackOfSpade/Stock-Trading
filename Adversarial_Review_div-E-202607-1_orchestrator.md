## (d) Verdict reasoning

**On ACTIVATE vs. DO-NOT-ACTIVATE.** The governing rule is Strategy.md:154(d)/156: the orchestrator produces a final activation state, which binds if the theater-check is DIVERGENT or MIXED. M1b's DO-NOT-ACTIVATE rests on three grounds. One (correlation-stationarity) is empirically refuted by the same-cycle M2 measurement, confirmed independently against the raw decision_log entry rather than the attacker's paraphrase. A second (energy-shock dispersion compression) is a capacity/thin-edge observation dressed as a mechanism-failure claim, and this cycle's own screen produced tradeable dispersion (COR/MCK, CB/TRV) at correlations well above the 0.5 floor, undercutting the idea that compression has reached opportunity-eliminating levels. The third (duration/style rotation) is real and untouched by measurement, but is exactly the failure mode Strategy E's own beta-adjustment clause is designed to absorb, so it does not stand as an unqualified veto either. None of the three grounds, individually or together, presents the kind of structural mechanism-failure argument that should override a live technical ACTIVATE (SPY UP, VIX NORMAL, breadth HEALTHY, curve NOT-SUSTAINED) for a market-neutral strategy whose entry/exit criteria already mechanically re-test the exact risk M1b worries about on every candidate pair — proven operative same-day, when NVDA/AMD was independently rejected on Entry criterion 3 regardless of router state. The common-cause warning (this is one regime disagreement refracted five ways) is real and I do not treat E's ACTIVATE as independent confirmation of A/B/C/D's calls, nor vice versa — but it does not, by itself, strengthen E's mechanism-specific fundamental case, which is what actually has to carry the DO-NOT-ACTIVATE call given no reconciliation override applies to E.

**On the execution-feasibility-deferred qualifier (the separable question).** The qualifier's own founding text (found via `events.regime_events`, not via the task's suggested path) shows it was doing double duty: an execution-mechanics gate AND an informal risk-buffer for the correlation-stationarity concern. Both halves are now substantively addressed: the mechanics half directly (fractional shorts confirmed enabled on the live account, commission immaterial at 0.42% of gross against a 15% ceiling, borrow immaterial on the sampled names), and the risk-buffer half by the same M2 measurement that refutes the stationarity-stress claim, backstopped by the fact that Strategy E's mechanical Entry criterion 3 (>=0.5 at entry) and exit correlation-breakdown rule (<0.3 triggers exit) continue to police exactly this risk on every individual pair regardless of whether the qualifier exists. Lifting the qualifier therefore does not remove a safeguard — it removes a redundant, coarser, strategy-wide proxy for a risk that a finer, always-on, per-pair mechanical control already covers. The residual points — preview-is-not-a-fill, leg-fill asymmetry on unmarketable limit orders, CRWD's individual SLB check, and the self-referential Rev-43 pre-mortem defect — are real, but each is narrow and name- or order-specific rather than book-wide, and the right place for them is ordinary per-trade execution diligence (which M2 was already doing pair-by-pair), not a standing strategy-level deferral. I therefore lift the qualifier rather than retain or merely narrow it, while explicitly directing that this diligence continue on every individual E order. The closest spec analog, `Strategy.md:1501`'s ETF-pair-substitution clause, is not the same mechanism — it is a standing structural accommodation keyed to portfolio-size growth, not a temporary, review-created deferral with a stated (if previously undisclosed) dual justification — so I do not treat that clause's text as governing this decision; I decide it on the qualifier's own founding rationale instead.
