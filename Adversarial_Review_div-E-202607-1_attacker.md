## Bear case against the M1b fundamental claim

M1b's Strategy E section builds its DO-NOT-ACTIVATE on three legs: an energy sector-wide shock said to compress intra-group dispersion, a duration/style rotation said to dominate long/short separation, and a claim that "the correlation-stationarity condition behind E's Entry criterion 3 (trailing-252-day correlation ≥ 0.5) is further stressed." That third leg is not merely weak — it is empirically refuted. M2's independent measurement finds 60-day correlation exceeding 252-day correlation in 66 of 89 pairs (74%): recent co-movement is *higher* than the long-run baseline, which is the opposite of a stationarity break. A trailing correlation series that is currently running hot relative to its own trailing average is not "stressed" in the direction M1b asserts; if anything it says the hedge is currently tighter than usual.

That refutation cuts deeper than losing one leg of three. M1b's own text ties this exact mechanism to a second, load-bearing claim: "the structural-hedge-failure-before-convergence risk that `div-E-202606-1` left un-refuted is materially higher under an acute shock." A hedge-failure risk driven by decorrelation should track *falling* correlation, not rising correlation — M2's finding does not just fail to support this claim, it points the other way. The artifact asserts a risk is "materially higher" using a mechanism that measured data shows moving in the opposite direction.

The meta-question is unavoidable: the DO-NOT-ACTIVATE call for E does not move even though one of its three stated grounds is empirically refuted and a second, dependent claim is undercut by the same data. If a stated ground can be knocked out entirely without moving the conclusion, either that ground was never actually load-bearing — in which case it should not have been presented as evidence for the call — or the call is constructed to survive any single refutation, which is the hallmark of an unfalsifiable conclusion rather than a reasoned one.

The remaining two legs are weaker than they look under mechanism scrutiny. E is a market-neutral pairs strategy; its own fundamental question is "whether macro forces are dominating intra-sector dynamics" — but the artifact never establishes that a common-factor shock hitting both legs of a properly matched pair together is a problem for a market-neutral structure. Common-factor moves that hit both legs equally are precisely what pair construction is designed to net out; the entire premise of trading the *within-group spread* rather than the group itself is that shared shocks should wash out of the L-S return. M1b's Energy paragraph asserts the shock "compress[es] exactly the intra-group dispersion E monetises" — but compression of the spread available to converge is a capacity/opportunity-sizing question (how much edge is on the table), not evidence that macro is "dominating" the mechanism the pair is built to be neutral to. M1b never runs that distinction; it treats a magnitude observation as if it settled a mechanism-failure question. And critically, the dispersion-compression finding is exactly what M2 *supports* — which should be read as evidence the intra-group hedges are behaving normally and tightly, not as evidence the underlying mechanism is broken.

The one leg genuinely untouched by any of this is the duration/style-rotation point (large-cap growth −6.57% vs. equal-weight +1.05%) — M2's finding says nothing about it, and it survives as an independent basis, which is why the fundamental call cannot be said to fully collapse. But two of the artifact's three stated grounds are now either refuted outright or resting on an unexamined inferential leap, in a review where the technical plane is unambiguously ACTIVATE (SPY UP, VIX NORMAL 15.99, breadth HEALTHY 67%, curve NOT-SUSTAINED) and the one operational objection previously blocking activation — execution feasibility — has been affirmatively discharged three separate ways this same cycle (fractional shorts permitted, round-trip commission corrected downward 5x to 0.42% against 5-10% expected convergence, borrow 0.25–0.43% with ample availability on a third of the sampled candidates). The case for treating E's DO-NOT-ACTIVATE as a robust, mechanism-grounded fundamental veto over a live technical ACTIVATE is substantially weaker than the artifact's confident single-sentence "Fundamental DO-NOT-ACTIVATE" presents it as.

## Weaknesses identified

1. **Refuted stationarity ground, unmoved conclusion** — M1b states: "The correlation-stationarity condition behind E's Entry criterion 3 (trailing-252-day correlation ≥ 0.5) is further stressed: a single month containing a ~20% oil round trip and a 6.6% style rotation is another regime break inside the trailing window." M2 measured 60d correlation exceeding 252d correlation in 66/89 pairs (74%), directly refuting "stressed." The DO-NOT-ACTIVATE call is unchanged by the loss of this ground, raising the question of whether it was ever load-bearing or whether the conclusion is unfalsifiable.

2. **Hedge-failure-risk claim likely runs backwards** — M1b states: "The structural-hedge-failure-before-convergence risk that `div-E-202606-1` left un-refuted is materially higher under an acute shock." This claim depends on the same correlation mechanism M2 refutes; since correlation is running *above* its trailing baseline in most pairs, the risk this sentence asserts as "materially higher" is, on the same data, more plausibly lower.

3. **Mechanism-specificity gap — common-factor shock vs. market-neutral construction** — M1b's Energy paragraph argues a sector-wide shock "moves both legs of an energy, transport, chemicals or industrials pair together — compressing exactly the intra-group dispersion E monetises" and treats this as support for DO-NOT-ACTIVATE, but never establishes why a shock hitting both matched legs together defeats a market-neutral, within-group-spread strategy rather than being exactly the kind of common-factor move pair construction is built to net out. The fundamental question as posed ("whether macro forces are dominating intra-sector dynamics") is answered by asserting magnitude and direction of the shock, not by testing whether it survives netting across the matched legs.

4. **Dispersion-compression inference is a capacity claim dressed as a risk claim** — M2 SUPPORTS the dispersion-compression measurement itself, and that support is conceded here. But M1b's use of it — "compressing exactly the intra-group dispersion E monetises" — argues thinner available spread, which is an opportunity-sizing observation (smaller edge per trade), not evidence of a broken mechanism requiring a macro-level DO-NOT-ACTIVATE. Tighter recent co-movement is, if anything, consistent with better hedge integrity at entry, the opposite of the framing M1b gives it.

5. **Execution-feasibility deferral carried forward without engaging its own-cycle resolution** — M1b's downstream note states the execution-feasibility gate is "unaffected by this routine," but the fundamental reasoning for E (Reasoning paragraph, Strategy E section) makes no reference to execution friction at all — it is argued entirely off correlation/dispersion mechanics. This confirms the deferral qualifier is severable from the fundamental call; nothing in the artifact's own fundamental reasoning is a basis for keeping the deferral attached now that fractional shorts, commission, and borrow have all been resolved affirmatively this cycle.

6. **Common-cause dilution, flagged by the artifact itself** — M1b's own text warns: "must NOT read five concurrent DIVERGENT verdicts as five independent confirmations... If the reviews converge, the honest reading is one regime call being made five times, not five findings." E's raw DO-NOT-ACTIVATE (unlike B's, which is override-driven) is one of four raw calls keyed to the same shared three-axis M1a regime read (growth decelerating, policy hawkish, shock acute); the artifact's own self-diagnosis undercuts treating E's fundamental call as independently corroborated evidence beyond that single shared regime input.

7. **Unfalsifiability meta-point** — taken together with #1, the conclusion (DO-NOT-ACTIVATE) survived the loss of a stated ground with zero adjustment to the call's confidence, framing, or the "Fundamental DO-NOT-ACTIVATE" one-line verdict at the end of the Reasoning paragraph — no hedge, no downgrade in stated certainty, nothing acknowledging that a plank has been kicked out from under it (because, at compile time 2026-08-03, M1b could not yet see the M2 finding — but that is exactly why this review, not M1b, is the correction point).
